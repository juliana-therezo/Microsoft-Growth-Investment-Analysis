# Where to Invest and What to Expect: Microsoft Capital Allocation and Q2 FY27 Revenue Forecast

**An end-to-end FP&A analytics project: messy finance exports → Python cleaning → SQL star schema (DuckDB) → return on capex, budget variance and a revenue forecast → Tableau dashboard.**

📊 **Interactive dashboard (Tableau Public):** [Microsoft: Where to Invest and What to Expect](https://public.tableau.com/app/profile/juliana.therezo/viz/Microsoft_Where_to_Invest/1_CFOoverview)

> ## ⚠️ All data in this project is synthetic, calibrated to Microsoft's published results
> Every number is generated by `data/generate.py`. Customers, transactions, prices, cost centres, datacenter
> projects, pipeline deals and FX rates are invented, and **no individual record is Microsoft data**.
> Only the totals are calibrated: revenue by segment and fiscal quarter, operating margins, operating-expense
> ratios and capital expenditures (including finance leases) are close to what Microsoft published for
> FY24 Q3 – FY27 Q1 (see the Calibration block in `generate.py`; FY26 revenue is $330.5B here vs $331.8B published).
> Product-line splits, regional and datacenter figures, FY26 Q3 – FY27 Q1 capex and the FY27 Q1 split into the
> three FY26 segments are estimates. The data is for a portfolio project and must not be used as information
> about the company. See [Limitations](#limitations).

## Recommendations

| # | Recommendation | Impact | Evidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Shift about $14.2B of FY27 datacenter capex** (including finance leases) from the 5 cloud regions that do not earn their cost to the 5 highest-return regions | **+$1.0B operating income a year** once capacity fills | Return on capex 8.6% vs 1.7%; payback 4.8 vs 7.5 years |
| 2 | **Optimise Azure through cost per MW**, not operating-expense cuts | Close the gap between Azure's 35% margin and the company's 47.3% | Azure grows fastest (+42.6% in FY26) and already shows operating leverage; the gap is cost of revenue (datacenter depreciation) |
| 3 | **Plan FY27 realistically**: budget R&D at the actual run-rate and Azure volume against available capacity | Avoid repeating the FY26 miss | FY26 operating income missed budget by $3.2B (−2.0%); R&D was 6.2% over budget ($34.6B vs $32.6B) |
| 4 | **Use $96.0B as the Q2 FY27 revenue base case** | 80% interval: $92.6B – $99.4B | Budget, run-rate + pipeline and last year's growth all fall inside the 80% interval |

**Decision requested:** approve the capex shift for the FY27 plan, review cost overruns in the five weak regions before approving new builds there, and use $96.0B as the Q2 FY27 base case.

## Key insights

### 1. Where to invest: growth vs margin
Each product line is placed on two axes: revenue growth (FY26 vs FY25, full fiscal years only) and operating margin after depreciation. The cut-offs were fixed before looking at the results: median growth of the 12 product lines (**8.5%**) and the company margin (**47.3%**).

| Action | Product lines | FY26 evidence |
|---|---|---|
| **Invest** | Microsoft 365 Commercial, Dynamics 365, Microsoft 365 Consumer | +17.6% to +19.8% growth, 50%–65% margin |
| **Optimise** | Azure, LinkedIn, Search and News Advertising | Azure +42.6% at a 35% margin; LinkedIn 39%, Search 32% |
| **Maintain** | Server Products, Windows OEM, Windows Commercial | 69%–78% margin, little or no growth: protect the cash |
| **Deprioritise** | Gaming, Devices, Enterprise and Partner Services | Gaming −5.7% at 10%; Devices −15.5% with a negative margin |

- **Regions:** North America (+19.6%, $103B) and Northern Europe (+22.1%) fall in Invest. By sales region, Americas = Invest, APAC = Maintain, EMEA = Deprioritise (EMEA is only 0.6 points below the margin cut-off, so that label is fragile).
- **Operating leverage:** every product line grew operating expense slower than revenue in FY26, so Azure's lower margin is a cost-of-revenue (depreciation) question, not an overhead one.
- **Opportunity sizing:** closing Gaming's margin gap to realistic peers is worth up to **$12.4B** a year. Azure's **$33.4B** is an upper bound only, because its peer target includes licence businesses with no datacenter costs.

### 2. Datacenter spending mostly pays back, but not everywhere
- Quarterly capex (including finance leases) tripled from **$14.0B to $45.2B** (FY24 Q3 → FY27 Q1); FY26 capex was **$155.9B**. About **a third** of it (26%–43% by quarter) is financed through leases, which is why free cash flow including leases fell to about $8.5B a quarter.
- **29 of 34** cloud regions earn the 9% cost of capital. NPV of all $322B of capex at 9%: **+$52.7B** (+$88.1B at 7%, +$21.9B at 11%). Median payback: **5.7 years**, inside the assets' useful life. 11 regions change verdict between 7% and 11%.
- The 5 regions that do not earn their cost: Norway East, Mexico Central, South Africa North, Italy North and Switzerland North (payback 6.4–11.3 years).
- **Cost per MW explains the return** (correlation −0.95), not utilisation (+0.21): $31–40M per MW in the best regions vs $48–85M in the weakest.

### 3. FY26 missed its profit plan by $3.2B, and costs drove it
- Revenue landed on plan (**$330.5B vs $330.1B**, +0.1%), but operating income missed by **$3.2B (−2.0%)**.
- Bridge: **$159.4B budget → volume −$1.8B → price/mix +$2.1B → cost −$3.5B → $156.2B actual.**
- R&D ran **5%–7% over budget in every segment** (+6.2% overall). By product line, Azure (−$2.9B, mostly volume below an aggressive plan) and Gaming (−$2.0B) drove the miss; Microsoft 365 Commercial's price/mix (+$3.7B) offset part of it.
- FY27 is back above plan: Q1 operating income **+$1.4B (+3.2%)** above budget.

### 4. What to expect: Q2 FY27 revenue of about $96.0B
| | Q2 FY27 revenue |
|---|---|
| **Forecast** | **$96.0B** (Oct $18.1B · Nov $19.5B · Dec $58.4B) |
| 80% interval | $92.6B – $99.4B |
| 95% interval | $90.8B – $101.2B |
| Cross-check: budget | $95.2B (−0.8%) ✔ inside 80% |
| Cross-check: run-rate + weighted pipeline | $94.0B (−2.1%) ✔ inside 80% |
| Cross-check: last year's growth | $96.2B (+0.2%) ✔ inside 80% |

- **Model:** log-linear trend with a fixed effect for each fiscal month (quarter-end billing makes Sep, Dec, Mar and Jun about three times a normal month).
- **Chosen by back-test** on Apr–Sep 2026: MAPE **2.5%** monthly (1.4% quarterly) vs **8.3%** for a linear trend.
- **Risk:** back-test errors lean positive (upside), but the pipeline covers only **63%** of the gap between the Q1 run-rate and the Q2 budget, so new business must close to reach the top half of the range.

## The business question

> Where should Microsoft put its next investment dollar (by segment, product line and cloud region) to add the most operating income, and what revenue should it expect in fiscal Q2 FY27 (Oct–Dec 2026)?

**Objective:** maximise operating income in USD. Growth and margin are the means. When they conflict, operating income decides.

| Decision | Owner | Output |
|---|---|---|
| Which product lines and regions get more investment | CFO, segment leaders | Ranked investment list ([03](notebooks/03_profitability.ipynb)) |
| Which datacenter regions earn their cost | Investment committee | Return on capex, payback and NPV per region ([04](notebooks/04_return_on_capex.ipynb)) |
| Where actuals miss plan, and why | CFO, FP&A | Variance bridge with drivers ([05](notebooks/05_budget_variance.ipynb)) |
| What revenue to expect in Q2 FY27 | CFO, Sales Director | Forecast with 80% and 95% intervals ([06](notebooks/06_forecast.ipynb)) |

Out of scope: share buybacks, dividends, acquisitions and tax.

## How it was built

```
generate.py ──► 8 raw CSVs ──► 01 Profile ──► 02 Clean ──► DuckDB star schema ──► 17 SQL views ──► 03–05 Analysis
   (Python)                     (Python)      (Python)         (SQL)               (SQL)          06 Forecast (Python)
                                                                                                   Tableau dashboard
```

| Step | Tool | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Generate | Python (numpy, pandas) | Seeded script writes 8 system-style exports, 470,382 rows (Jan 2024 – Sep 2026) |
| Profile | Python (pandas) | Row counts, gaps, duplicates, date and number formats per file |
| Clean | Python (pandas) | Fixes the 14 issues found in profiling ([profiling_issues.csv](reports/profiling_issues.csv)) in 21 logged steps ([cleaning_log.csv](data/clean/cleaning_log.csv)) |
| Model | SQL (DuckDB) | Star schema: billing, costs, depreciation, capex and capacity, budget and pipeline facts around shared dimensions |
| Analyse | SQL (DuckDB) | 17 views (`v_params` + 16 analysis views): every KPI is defined once and read by the notebooks and Tableau |
| Forecast | Python (pandas, numpy) | Back-tested model with prediction intervals and three independent cross-checks |
| Present | Tableau Public | 5-page interactive dashboard for non-technical decision-makers |

**Why these tools:** Python for messy parsing and the forecast; SQL (DuckDB) to join and aggregate 470k rows in seconds with no server and define each KPI exactly once; Tableau so directors can filter and drill down without code.

### Key decisions and why
1. **Full fiscal years only** for growth, so a half year is never compared with a full one.
2. **Restated a product line that changed segment** (Windows Commercial, July 2025) for like-for-like comparisons.
3. **Finance leases count as capex** (as Microsoft reports it). About a third of datacenter spend is leased; ignoring it overstates returns.
4. **Portfolio cut-offs fixed in advance** (median growth, company margin), so the line cannot be moved after seeing results.
5. **All assumptions in one SQL view** (`v_params`): 9% cost of capital with 7% and 11% sensitivity, asset useful lives and target utilisation.
6. **Forecast model chosen by back-test**, not by preference.

## How to reproduce

**Requirements:** Python 3.10+.

```bash
pip install numpy pandas duckdb matplotlib seaborn jupyter
```

**1. Rebuild the raw data.** The seed is fixed (42), so every run produces identical files (about 60 seconds).

```bash
cd data
python generate.py
cd ..
```

The fiscal calendar (July–June) is not exported: it is built in SQL from the month dates. Two raw files need special read options:

```python
import pandas as pd

billing = pd.read_csv("data/raw/erp_billing_lines.csv", sep=";", dtype=str)   # ';' separator, decimal comma
budget = pd.read_csv("data/raw/fpa_budget.csv", skiprows=2)                   # 2 metadata lines, wide format
```

See [data/DATA_MODEL.md](data/DATA_MODEL.md) (section 6) for every format and data-quality issue to clean.

**2. Profile and clean.** Run `notebooks/01_profiling.ipynb` and `02_cleaning.ipynb` (writes `data/clean/` and the cleaning log).

**3. Model and analyse.** Run `notebooks/03_profitability.ipynb`: it builds `data/finance.duckdb` from `sql/01_build_star_schema.sql` and `sql/02_analysis_kpis.sql`. The SQL also runs on its own: `duckdb data/finance.duckdb -c ".read sql/02_analysis_kpis.sql"`.

**4. Return on capex, budget variance and forecast.** Run `notebooks/04` to `06`.

## Limitations

- All records are synthetic; only totals are calibrated to Microsoft's published results. Product-line splits, regional and datacenter figures, FY26 Q3 – FY27 Q1 capex and the FY27 Q1 segment split are estimates.
- From FY27 Microsoft reports new segments; this project keeps the three FY26 segments (Productivity and Business Processes, Intelligent Cloud, More Personal Computing).
- The 9% cost of capital is a stated assumption, tested at 7% and 11%.
- The forecast is back-tested on two quarters only, so the interval matters as much as the point estimate.
- Regional margins differ only through product mix, because costs are not booked by customer country.
